# Deep Research Prompt — Structural Feasibility and Folding Physics

You are a rigid-body simulation engineer, computational origami researcher, contact-mechanics specialist, magnetics engineer, numerical-methods architect, and educational visualization designer.

**Current date:** [insert current date]

Create a deeply cited technical dossier titled:

**“From Valid Net to Stable Build: Folding, Collision, Magnets, and Structural Confidence in MagnaMath”**

## Goal

Specify a progressive physical simulation that can distinguish a mathematically valid net from a kinematically reachable, collision-free, closeable, and plausibly stable magnetic-tile structure without pretending a browser simulation is a certified engineering analysis.

## Research questions

1. What minimum piece geometry is needed: thickness, bevel, corner radius, magnet envelope, hinge offset, and shell collision volume?
2. How should a face-tree folding motion be parameterized?
3. Which broad-phase and narrow-phase collision algorithms fit browser/WebAssembly constraints?
4. How should self-collision, allowed hinge contact, and final closure be distinguished?
5. How can loop-closing contacts be handled when a tree-folded net becomes a closed shell?
6. Which build orders reduce obstruction and inaccessible final joins?
7. What simplified magnet model is sufficient for attraction, detachment, and stability ranking?
8. How should gravity, tabletop support, friction, center of mass, and temporary bracing be modeled?
9. What confidence tiers can be validated against observed physical builds?
10. Which quantities should never be presented as precise without lab measurements?

## Required outputs

1. Literature and software comparison.
2. Progressive fidelity ladder: geometric, kinematic, collision, quasi-static, empirical.
3. State model and equations.
4. Piece/hinge/contact data contracts.
5. Solver and time-step recommendation for web and native clients.
6. Build-order optimization formulation.
7. Validation protocol against reference builds.
8. Failure taxonomy and user-facing language.
9. Performance budgets and graceful degradation.
10. Implementation milestones and tests.

Include pseudocode and explain how exact geometry state remains separate from approximate physics state.
