# Deep Research Prompt — Learning Science for Dynamic Geometry, Folding, and Physical Build-Along

You are a mathematics education researcher, spatial-reasoning researcher, cognitive scientist, constructionist-learning specialist, Montessori-informed educational designer, child-development researcher, accessibility specialist, and learning analytics architect.

Current date: 2026-08-10.

## Context

MagnaMath connects exact 3D solids, 2D nets, rigid folding, measurements, printable artifacts, and physical magnetic-tile builds. The intended audience ranges from elementary-aged children to technical adults. The same mechanism can support open exploration, guided lessons, physical play, and advanced mathematical inspection.

## Research objective

Design an evidence-grounded pedagogy and lesson architecture that uses dynamic folding and synchronized physical construction to improve spatial reasoning and geometry understanding without introducing visual or cognitive overload.

## Required research questions

1. What evidence exists for learning from dynamic geometry environments?
2. What evidence exists for folding/unfolding tasks and net-to-solid spatial reasoning?
3. How do manipulatives and virtual manipulatives compare and complement each other?
4. Which misconceptions commonly occur around faces, edges, vertices, nets, surface area, volume, dihedral angles, symmetry, Euler characteristic, and topology?
5. How should prediction, manipulation, explanation, and reflection be sequenced?
6. When does animation help versus create transient-information overload?
7. How should camera motion and visual highlighting be constrained for learning?
8. What age/developmental progressions are appropriate?
9. How can the same lesson support a child-friendly metaphor and an engineer-accurate explanation?
10. What role should embodied action, gesture, and physical build-along play?
11. How should incorrect physical builds be diagnosed without discouraging exploration?
12. What accessible alternatives support learners with low vision, motor differences, dyscalculia, ADHD, or autism?
13. What should be measured as learning outcomes rather than engagement proxies?
14. How can privacy-preserving progress and teacher views work?
15. What lesson-authoring schema would support reusable interactions and evidence?

## Required deliverables

1. Evidence synthesis with strength and limitations.
2. Learning progression by concept and approximate developmental readiness.
3. Misconception catalog and corrective interaction patterns.
4. Lesson pattern library, including predict–fold–explain, compare nets, build and verify, and debug a misconception.
5. Guidance for animation pacing, camera control, signaling, and learner control.
6. Physical/virtual integration model.
7. Accessibility and neurodiversity guidance.
8. Proposed lesson-content schema and event/marker model.
9. Assessment strategy with validated or defensible measures.
10. Example 12-lesson starter sequence.
11. Usability and learning-study plan.
12. Primary-source table with publication dates and links.
13. Claims requiring classroom experimentation.

## Constraints

- Do not infer learning from time-on-task alone.
- Do not assume more animation or interactivity is always better.
- Distinguish near transfer, far transfer, and motivation.
- Avoid rigid age gating where evidence supports flexible readiness.
